Predicting 30-day hospital readmission among patients with diabetes

How accurately can patient demographics, hospitalization history, and clinical characteristics predict 30-day hospital readmission among patients with diabetes?

Research overview

Early readmission can signal unmet needs after discharge. Identifying encounters at elevated risk could support follow-up planning. This study evaluates whether routinely collected information can distinguish 30-day readmissions from other outcomes.

Dataset and predictors

Study cohort

UCI Diabetes 130-US Hospitals (1999–2008)

101,766 original encounters; 2,423 excluded for death or hospice-related discharge dispositions. The binary outcome is readmission within 30 days versus all other recorded readmission outcomes.

14 predictors

Demographics: Age, gender, race.

Prior utilization: Inpatient, emergency and outpatient visits.

Clinical characteristics: Hospital stay, lab procedures, procedures, medications, diagnoses, A1C result, insulin status and medication change.

Missing race: 2.25%; missing A1C: 83.05%. Retained as Unknown and Not measured, respectively.

Analysis process

  1. Load the UCI data, define the binary outcome, and exclude death/hospice dispositions.
  2. Choose 14 predictors; retain missing race and A1C as separate categories.
  3. Split by patient ID: 79,541 training and 19,802 testing encounters; zero overlapping patients.
  4. Standardize numerical features and one-hot encode categorical features in model pipelines fitted on training data.
  5. Fit Logistic Regression, Random Forest (200 trees, depth 10, minimum leaf 30), and Gradient Boosting (100 trees, learning rate 0.05, depth 3).
  6. Compare AUROC using five patient-grouped training folds; select Random Forest and a threshold of 0.12 using training validation, then evaluate on held-out data.

Sensitivity–specificity trade-off

● Sensitivity   ● Specificity

The chosen cutoff of 0.12 targets detection of approximately half of actual readmissions while maintaining reasonable specificity.

Held-out test results at 0.12: 67.82% accuracy, 47.56% sensitivity, 70.37% specificity and 0.6352 AUROC. Of 2,216 actual readmissions, 1,054 were identified and 1,162 missed; 5,211 other encounters were falsely flagged.

Conclusion and limitations

Routinely collected demographics, hospitalization history and clinical features offer modest discrimination of 30-day readmission, better than random chance. Random Forest performed best by AUROC, but its advantage was small.

Lowering the threshold improved sensitivity at the cost of more false positives. The model missed more than half of actual readmissions. Historical data, substantial missing A1C information, modest discrimination, and lack of external validation limit applicability.

Educational research only. This dashboard is not validated for clinical decision-making and should not guide individual care.
